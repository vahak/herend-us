A Collector's Guide to Herend Porcelain Figurines

Herend figurines are small animals in hand-painted, hard-paste porcelain, made in Herend, a small town near Veszprém in western Hungary, where the manufactory was founded in 1826. Most are covered in fishnet, a fine net of painted scales finished with real gold, and they are painted with squirrel-hair brushes. This guide explains how to identify, choose, look after and collect them.

Written by Tamar Medzadourian, Paris Tableware, Ridgewood, New Jersey. We sell Herend online at paristableware.com.

Herend in 60 seconds

Vince Stingl founded the works at Herend in 1826, and Mór Fischer took over in 1839. At London's Great Exhibition in 1851, Queen Victoria ordered a butterfly-and-flower service that still carries her name. In 1872 the manufactory became Supplier to the Imperial and Royal Court, and according to Herend's own timeline, the 1933 World's Fair in Chicago marked the beginning of Herend exports to the United States. What makes Herend figurines easy to collect

Three details help a collector know exactly which piece they have, and which one to add next.

  • Colors with names and codes. Each fishnet color has a name and a decor code, such as Blue (VHB), Raspberry (VHP) or Key Lime (VHV1). Of the 749 Herend figurine models we had in stock on October 24, 2026, 486 were offered in more than one color.
  • A model number that stays the same. The five-digit model number is the same in every color, so you can match a piece you already own. Our figurine guide shows how to read the full item number.
  • Painting by hand. Because each piece is painted by hand, two figurines of the same model and color differ slightly in the net, the gold touches and the eyes.

How to check that a seller has genuine Herend

A backstamp can be copied, so the mark alone is not proof. Three habits lower the risk:

  1. Buy from an established retailer. Look for a store with a street address, a phone number that someone answers and a written return policy, and ask how long it has sold Herend. Paris Tableware is an authorized Herend retailer. Its store FAQ says so, naming Herend among the brands it carries.
  2. Check the backstamp. When the piece arrives, turn it over. Our guide to Herend marks explains the trademark, shape number and decor code, and the decor code should match the color named in the listing.
  3. Be wary of prices far below retail. Herend is shaped, painted and gilded by hand. A new piece offered far below its usual retail price is a reason to ask more questions before you buy.

Common questions

Is Herend porcelain hand painted?

Yes. Herend's patterns and fishnet figurines are painted by hand with squirrel-hair brushes, then fired again at about 800°C (about 1,470°F) so the decoration fires onto the surface, according to the manufactory's own description of its process. The gilding is real gold.

Where is Herend made?

In Herend, a small town near Veszprém in western Hungary, where the works were founded in 1826. According to Herend's own timeline, its exports to the United States began with the 1933 World's Fair in Chicago.

How many colors do Herend figurines come in?

Our Herend catalog lists ten fishnet colors: Blue, Raspberry, Key Lime, Green, Rust, Black, Butterscotch, Sapphire, Chocolate and Gold. Not every model comes in every color. On October 24, 2026, 486 of the 749 Herend figurine models we stocked were offered in more than one color.

How can I tell if a Herend figurine is genuine?

A mark alone is not proof, because a backstamp can be copied. Buy from an established retailer with a street address, a phone number and a written return policy, check that the decor code on the base matches the color you ordered, and be wary of a new piece priced far below retail.
